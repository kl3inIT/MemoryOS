# MEM-82 — Vadan public landing page

## Problem

Vadan needs a public website at `https://vadan.app` that presents MemoryOS for two uses: the company-website field of the AWS Activate application, and the link sent with the AWS Production PoC infrastructure/credit brief ([MEM-80](https://linear.app/memory-os/issue/MEM-80)). No public page exists. `web/` is the per-customer self-hosted application whose `/` is authenticated Chat, and [MEM-13](../../completed/mem-13-production-web-foundation/design.md) deliberately kept marketing out of it.

Linear: [MEM-82](https://linear.app/memory-os/issue/MEM-82).

## Visual direction

The product owner asked for a technical look in the manner of Laravel Cloud. Hanken Grotesk and the product's token names stay; the values become a navy-and-blue identity: a near-black navy ground in the dark theme, a cool light ground in the light theme, and one light source — a blue glow behind the hero's cited-answer preview, echoed softly behind the closing call to action. The preview stays the one emphasized element, because a verifiable answer is what MemoryOS delivers. Brand blue marks the product's own moving parts: citations, the access check, and the markers of real sequences (the ingestion stages and the track's progress). Approval green is used only for an approved AI asset. Headings stay left-aligned without labels above them. Sections carry no decorative icons, cards or boxed mocks: type carries them, after gsap.com. On the light ground the glow uses the brand blue at a strength that still reads as blue; the first light glow looked washed out to the product owner.

One type scale serves the page (`src/styles/theme.css`). Each step grows linearly between a 390 px and a 1440 px viewport and is about one and a half times the step below it, so a section's title always outranks the entries in it: display (44–72 px) for the hero headline and the stage titles; the section heading (34–56 px); the entry title (24–36 px), with the statement as its text weight; and the item heading (18–22 px) for questions and labels, with the lead as its text weight for descriptions. Body and interface text keep the web app's presets. Labels — the stage numbers and titles, the access gate's three parties, the asset's Released mark and the delivery surfaces — are type on a tinted fill in the citation, accent or approval colours.

Motion carries the explanation, and answers the visitor's own actions. Switching the theme grows the new theme in a circle from the toggle (View Transitions API). On load the headline sets itself letter by letter: each letter rises out of its word from the baseline, in reading order, and the positioning paragraph's words follow, while the actions and the preview rise in beneath them. The headline's split is undone once it has landed. 6.0 first typed a one-sentence statement; the positioning paragraph is too long to type. Scrolling drives the rest through GSAP ScrollTrigger, in both directions:

- **Product** draws each highlight's lines as the drawing crosses the viewport; once drawn, packets keep travelling its lines and the access check pulses while the section is on screen.
- **How it works** lets the type carry the story, after gsap.com, whose headings are large, tight and even rather than decorated. As a stage arrives, its number and title flip up on a hinge along their top edge and settle with a spring, one after the other, and the words of its sentence rise out of their masks (SplitText, `src/motion/text.ts`); the entrance plays once and reverses when the visitor scrolls back above it. From lg the list pins once it reaches the middle of the viewport, keeping its own height and the space above it, and slides sideways one to one with the scroll, one stage at a time, while the next stage waits at the right edge and a counter and hairline show the progress (`src/motion/track.ts`); it is the one pinned scene. The access gate then reveals the same way: the hairline joining its three columns draws from the person to the model, the labels flip up, the passages arrive and the blocked ones are struck through.
- **Capabilities** and **Organizational AI Memory** reveal each block the same way as it crosses the viewport. From lg the capabilities' sticky index marks the capability crossing the middle of the viewport.
- The hero glow drifts away, and canvas particle fields drift behind the hero and the closing call to action; they pause off screen and in background tabs.

Only how it works pins, and only from lg; every other scene scrubs or plays in place. The markup always holds the final state: GSAP sets start states only while `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference` matches, so reduced motion, a failed script and the component tests all render the complete static page — how it works then stays a stacked list, because only motion sets the `data-track` attribute that lays it out in a row. Only transient marks — the drawings' packets — are hidden in the markup.

Motion sets up after the first frame, each section in a task of its own. Setting up reads styles and layout; done while the page mounts, it made the browser lay out the whole page inside that task, and Lighthouse mobile performance fell to 0.78 (TBT 750 ms). The hero's start state must be in the first frame, so before that frame it only sets `data-intro="pending"`, which CSS reads to hide the headline, description, actions and preview until its sequence takes over.

Splitting text added a second cost. GSAP reads an element's transform from its computed style the first time it animates it, and a `from` tween writes each target's start before reading the next, so each of the 608 fresh SplitText pieces forced a layout as the page set up, and Lighthouse mobile fell to 0.74 (TBT 1040 ms). `splitText` (`src/motion/text.ts`) reads every piece's transform in one pass right after the split, which costs one layout, and GSAP keeps the values for its tweens: 0.94 to 0.96, with TBT 130 to 190 ms ([verification](verification.md#type-across-the-page)).

## Technical design

- `landing/` is a standalone pnpm package (own lockfile, not a workspace member of `web/`). It uses the same pinned versions as `web/` for React, Vite, TypeScript, Tailwind, lucide-react, clsx/tailwind-merge, Hanken Grotesk, Vitest, Testing Library, oxlint and oxfmt. There is no router, query client, API client, Radix or class-variance-authority. lucide-react remains for interface controls only (menu, theme toggle, disclosure, lock and checks).
- GSAP 3.15.0 and `@gsap/react` 2.1.2 (exact pins, bundled, so the strict CSP holds: GSAP writes styles through the CSSOM) drive the motion. `src/motion/motion.ts` registers ScrollTrigger and exposes `useMotion`, which runs each section's setup after the first frame, in page order, inside one `gsap.matchMedia` context keyed on reduced motion, width and height; unmounting or a media change reverts it to the static markup. `allowsMotion` answers the same reduced-motion query for the hero's pre-frame attribute. `src/motion/text.ts` splits text (`splitText`) and plays the flip-label and rising-word entrance (`revealText`); `src/motion/track.ts` runs the pinned horizontal track. `src/components/particle-field.tsx` draws the canvas fields. The Product drawings share `Drawing`, `Packet` and `PageGlyph` from `src/components/line-drawing.tsx` and the `ramp` style helper from `src/lib/ramp.ts`; `src/motion/drawings.ts` scrubs each drawing's `--p` and marks finished drawings live. `src/styles/base.css` holds the `ramp` utilities, the drawings' ambient loops and the hero's pre-frame rule.
- `src/content.ts` owns every string, link and list; section components under `src/sections/` render it. The exception is the sample data inside the decorative, `aria-hidden` Product drawings — their question and labels — which lives with its drawing and only restates what the visible copy says. Shared primitives live under `src/components/`: `ActionLink` (default-tone rows of the action matrix as links), `Section` (landmark, heading and description), `BrandMark`, `VadanLogo` (the company wordmark as a single-colour mask) and `ParticleField`.
- `src/styles/tokens.css` keeps the `web/` semantic token names and default-tone action rows (source: `web/src/styles/tokens.css`) with the landing's own navy/blue values for light and `.dark`, plus `accent`, `glow` and `brand-mark` tokens; `src/styles/base.css` holds the two decorative glow layers. It is a deliberate copy rather than an import: the application and the marketing site deploy independently, and a cross-package import would couple their build contexts. `src/styles/theme.css` maps them for Tailwind and adds the landing type scale next to the web typography presets.
- The theme follows the web app's contract: the `dark` class on `<html>` and the `memoryos-theme` storage key (`light` or `dark`; absent means the system preference). `public/theme-init.js`, a classic same-origin script at the top of `<head>`, applies it before first paint, so the strict CSP needs no inline script and dark-mode visitors never see a light flash. `src/lib/theme.ts` owns the toggle, follows system changes until the visitor chooses, and falls back to the system preference when the browser blocks storage. The Open Graph image stays light.
- `index.html` owns the title, description, canonical URL, Open Graph/Twitter tags, and JSON-LD for `Organization` (Vadan, `funder` GenAI Fund), `SoftwareApplication` (MemoryOS) and `WebSite`. `public/` holds `favicon.svg`, `og-image.png` (1200 × 630, rendered from `scripts/og-image.html`), `vadan-logo.png` (the JSON-LD logo, a navy copy of the wordmark), `robots.txt` and `sitemap.xml`.
- The page renders on the client. Prerendering is not added: reviewers use browsers, crawlers that execute JavaScript see the full page, and the static head carries the metadata. Revisit only if search visibility becomes a requirement.

## Delivery

- `landing/Dockerfile` builds from the `landing/` directory as its own context (with `landing/.dockerignore`) using `node:24-alpine`, and serves with the same pinned `nginx:1.31-alpine` as `web/` as UID 101 on port 8080, with OCI revision/source labels. nginx starts directly; the image's entrypoint scripts are not needed.
- `landing/nginx.conf` serves `/`, the hashed `/assets/` and the public files, returns 404 for unknown paths, answers `GET /healthz` for the container check, compresses text assets, and sets a strict CSP (`default-src 'self'; script-src 'self'; style-src 'self'; font-src 'self'; img-src 'self' data:; connect-src 'self'; frame-ancestors 'none'; base-uri 'self'; form-action 'none'`) plus `X-Content-Type-Options`, `Referrer-Policy`, `Permissions-Policy` and `X-Frame-Options`. Hashed assets are immutable; HTML is `no-cache`. HSTS is set at Nginx Proxy Manager, where TLS terminates.
- `infrastructure/deployment/compose.landing.yaml` defines one read-only `landing` service (`memoryos-landing`, `cap_drop: ALL`, `no-new-privileges`, tmpfs, small CPU/memory limits, healthcheck, no host port) on the external `proxy-network`. It runs as Compose project `memoryos-landing`, so the application deployment script never stops, pulls or rolls it back.
- CI adds a `landing` job to `CI Gate`: install, `pnpm --dir landing check`, image build, [`landing/scripts/smoke-image.sh`](../../../../landing/scripts/smoke-image.sh) against the built image, and Compose validation. On main it preserves the image as the `landing-image` artifact, outside the `candidate-*` pattern the application publication downloads. A `Publish landing` job after the gate pushes `ghcr.io/kl3init/memoryos-landing` and records the digest in the job summary and the `landing-release-<sha>-<attempt>` artifact. The application release bundle keeps its three-image contract.
- Deployment is an operator step documented in `docs/runbooks/landing.md`: Hostinger DNS (apex and `www` A records to the VPS, parking record removed, MX/TXT untouched), the Nginx Proxy Manager proxy host with Let's Encrypt, HSTS and a `www` redirection host, the digest-pinned `docker compose up --wait`, and rollback to the previous digest.

## Verification

- Component tests (`src/App.test.tsx`): one `h1`; every in-page anchor resolves to an element `id`; every `mailto:` targets `info@vadan.app`; every new-tab link carries `rel="noopener noreferrer"`; no link to the source repository or third-party notices; the rendered page names no internal technology (Docling, OCR, OpenSearch, PostgreSQL, Redis, MinIO, Python); every image, SVG and canvas has an accessible name or is hidden as decorative; every ingestion stage and capability has a heading and its visible description; the access gate lists the blocked and the permitted passages; without motion no element carries an inline opacity, transform or visibility, and no motion state (`data-intro`, `data-live`, `data-visible`) is set. Theme hook tests (`src/lib/theme.test.ts`): a stored theme is applied, and toggling applies and remembers the visitor's choice.
- Metadata test (`tests/site-metadata.test.mjs`): the JSON-LD in `index.html` parses and names Vadan, MemoryOS, GenAI Fund and the contact email; the canonical, `og:image`, `twitter:image` and logo URLs are absolute `https://vadan.app` URLs whose files exist in `public/`.
- `pnpm --dir landing check` (lint, format, tests, production build with font-asset assertion, TypeScript).
- Image: `smoke-image.sh` runs the image read-only with no capabilities and checks UID 101, `/`, `/healthz`, an unknown path (404), the public files, every security header and immutable asset caching. CI runs it on every change.
- Browser: inspect 390, 768, 1024 and 1440 px widths in both themes, scroll every scrubbed scene in both directions, watch the Product drawings' loops start and stop, check the static page with reduced motion, and run Lighthouse (mobile) against the container and the deployed URL; performance stays at or above 90 with CLS 0.
- Deployed: `https://vadan.app` returns 200 with the security headers and HSTS, `www` redirects, unknown paths return 404, and MX records are unchanged.
